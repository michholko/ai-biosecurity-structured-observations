# Structured Observations in AI-Biosecurity Research

Supplementary materials for:

> Holko, M., and Hu, B. (2026). *Configuration, Proxy Failure, and the Changing Unit of Biosecurity Analysis: Structured Observations Across Artificial Intelligence and the Future of Biosecurity, Volume I.* Frontiers in Microbiology. [DOI to be added upon publication]

## What this is

This repository contains the observational instrument, coded observations, synthesis, and reuse materials developed for a General Commentary on the first volume of the Frontiers Research Topic *Artificial Intelligence and the Future of Biosecurity* (RT 72195).

The commentary applies a structured observation instrument — a fixed set of nine analytical questions — consistently across 14 papers, with the goal of making findings comparable across contributions that do not share vocabulary, methodology, or disciplinary home. The instrument is offered as a reusable tool, not as a validated methodology: its claim to usefulness rests on whether it produces comparable, falsifiable observations across heterogeneous papers, not on the specific patterns it surfaces in any one application.

## How to reuse this instrument

The instrument is designed to be applied to any paper making claims about AI-enabled biosecurity. To apply it:

1. Read S1 to understand the nine dimensions, evidence classification, and the emergence test.
2. Use S4 (blank template) to code a new paper — fill in all nine dimensions, including nulls where a dimension is not addressed.
3. Tag each observation with an evidence relation (E1/E2/E3) and evidence basis (empirical/modeled/conceptual/normative-policy/review-derived).
4. Apply the emergence test to any capability, vulnerability, or protective effect you are characterizing as system-level rather than component-level.
5. Optionally use S5 (reuse prompt) to generate a first-pass structured observation with AI assistance, then review and correct it against the primary source.

Key discipline: observe first, synthesize second. Do not name cross-paper patterns during the coding pass (Level 1). Only after all papers are coded should you ask what patterns appear across them (Level 2).

## Corpus

**Corpus A** — submissions to the Frontiers Research Topic *Artificial Intelligence and the Future of Biosecurity* (RT 72195), selected by open call. Holko (2026, *Toward relational biosecurity*) is excluded from the coded corpus and used only as the analytic lens.

**Corpus B** — five published papers selected before coding on the criterion that each contributes an observation type absent from Corpus A:

- Wittmann et al. (2025). Strengthening nucleic acid biosecurity screening against generative protein design tools. *Science* 390(6768):82–87. doi: 10.1126/science.adu8578
- Bloomfield et al. (2024). AI and biosecurity: the need for governance. *Science* 385(6711):831–833. doi: 10.1126/science.adq1977
- Cai, Jeyapragasan, Nedungadi, Yukich & Donoughe (2025). Agentic BAIM–LLM Evaluation (ABLE). NeurIPS 2025 Workshop.
- Weidener et al. (2026). RefusalBench: Why Refusal Rate Misranks Frontier LLMs on Biological Research Prompts. arXiv:2605.21545.
- Liu, Nedungadi, Cai, Kleinman, Bhasin & Donoughe (2026). ABC-Bench: An Agentic Bio-Capabilities Benchmark for Biosecurity. arXiv:2606.11150.

## Status and limitations

This is a working instrument at an early stage. It has been applied once, by a single analyst, to a corpus selected through two non-random processes (a themed Research Topic call and analyst selection). It has not been peer-reviewed as a methodology in its own right, has not been tested for inter-rater reliability, and has not been validated on a representative sample of the AI-biosecurity literature. The instrument's claim to usefulness rests on whether it produces comparable observations across papers that share no common vocabulary — not on the specific patterns it reveals in this application.
